Why this question exists

Background: How We Got Here

The pool deck reconstruction is part of a broader ~$20 million program already funded through prior Special Assessments and reserves. The original permit allows rebuilding the deck in its existing configuration — that work is already under way.

During construction, engineers discovered that the 50-year-old structural slab cannot safely carry the weight of the existing planter and finish layout without extensive reinforcement — a potentially multi-million-dollar addition to the project.

To avoid that cost, the Board worked with engineers and designers to revise the layout, repositioning planters onto areas with adequate structural capacity while maintaining green space throughout the deck. This revised layout is the subject of this vote.

Implementation options

Phasing and Funding Position

Option A: Execute added amenities as part of current cycle

  • Lower remobilization and rework risk compared with later retrofits.
  • Better coordination while site teams are already mobilized.
  • Potentially more efficient commissioning and schedule integration.

Option B: Defer added amenities, prepare now

  • Enable structural/surface readiness now to minimize future disruption.
  • Spa zone can operate as elevated lounge deck if spa vessel is deferred.
  • Splash zone can function as normal deck amenity area if splash system is deferred.
  • Future installation may include remobilization and inefficiency premium.

Current funding direction (informational)

  • The current Board position is that owners are not expected to need an additional special assessment to fund spa/splash added elements.
  • Expected sources discussed are available funds within Capital Improvements reserve line items and/or savings achieved in Special Assessment-funded project execution.
  • Any final implementation timing and funding commitment remains subject to Board approval, legal/financial controls, and final procurement outcomes.

If approved

The new layout moves forward

The project will require a permit revision to be submitted to the City reflecting the new layout and proceed with the redesigned deck program. This layout reduces structural reinforcement needs by repositioning planters to previously reinforced areas, and enables the integrated amenity program (spa, splash, entry continuity) to be built within the current construction cycle.

If not approved

The original layout must be restored under the existing permit

The project would need to continue under the existing permit, which requires restoring the original planter positions and tile loads after the extensive reinforcement work is concluded. Based on the estimates, that reinforcement work alone could cost well in excess of $1,000,000 — and potentially significantly more.

This is not intended to pressure any owner's decision. It is factual context so that all owners understand the practical and financial implications of both paths. You can see reinforcement design and quotes in the project's folder in BuildingLink.

Frequently asked questions

FAQ

Are owners voting on exact final dollars in this question?

No. Owners are approving alteration/improvement direction. Final contracting values are determined through bids, permitting, and Board approvals.

Do owners need additional special assessment funds for spa/splash?

Current Board direction is no additional special assessment is expected for those added amenities, based on current funding assumptions and subject to final Board action.

Can added amenities be done later?

Yes. The page outlines a deferred path with enabling preparation now to reduce rework risk later.

Why include entry/rotonda tile continuity in this same question?

Because the arrival corridor and pool deck are visually connected. The intent is a coherent final aesthetic language across both areas.

Why is the proposed layout different from the original permit?

During construction, engineers found that the 50-year-old deck cannot support the original planter and tile loads without extensive reinforcement. The Board tasked the engineering and landscape teams to develop a new layout that avoids most of that reinforcement by repositioning planters to previously reinforced areas. This will imply a new reprogramming of the pool deck.

Why are the spa and splash-play area included in this proposal?

The revised layout frees up meaningful usable space on the deck by reducing the planter footprint. Adding these amenities now makes better use of that open area, can be done more efficiently while the project is already under construction, and helps avoid the higher cost and disruption of adding them later as a separate project.

Why is there a single proposal instead of multiple design options?

Developing a realistic proposal requires significant design, engineering, coordination, and review. Rather than present several speculative alternatives, the Board chose to bring forward one proposal that has already been studied and can realistically be implemented. Multiple competing versions would also split owner support across several options, making approval much less likely even if there is broad interest in moving the project forward.

Why did the Board include the new amenities in the same proposal instead of asking separately?

The Board concluded that a single, cohesive plan was the clearest and most practical approach. Because a permit revision is already required for the redesigned layout, this is the most efficient moment to evaluate the spa and splash-play area as part of the overall program. A more fragmented set of questions was considered, but combining the elements into one proposal was viewed as the better way to present a complete vision and take advantage of the lower implementation cost available now.

What happens if this question is not approved?

The project would continue under the existing permit, which requires restoring the original planter positions and tile loads. Because the slab cannot support those loads as-is, structural reinforcement would be required. Preliminary estimates place that cost well in excess of $1,000,000.

Should owners submit their Limited Proxy even if they disagree?

Yes. Submitting your Limited Proxy ensures your position is recorded in the official tabulation.
